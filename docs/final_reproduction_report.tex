\begin{abstract}
We independently reconstruct a published GPU symmetric Gauss--Seidel
Halpern-accelerated proximal-reflection method for multi-period DC optimal
power flow without the authors' code or instances. The canonical LP,
iteration, stopping maps, structural equality solve, CPU oracle, and resident
DGX Spark GPU implementation are validated. A sign inconsistency in the
printed rank-one inverse is corrected: maximum relative error falls to
$3.03\times10^{-16}$ from median error 0.206. Public MATPOWER models reproduce
all 18 reported dimensions but none of the nonzero counts. The GPU validates
11 allocated cases and the CPU 10; larger cases meet time, memory, or
sparse-index limits. These results establish structural---not exact or
performance---reproduction; no A100-equivalent speedup is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
DC optimal power flow, Halpern iteration, proximal reflection, symmetric
Gauss--Seidel decomposition, GPU sparse linear algebra, reproducibility, DGX
Spark.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}

Wang \emph{et al.} propose a GPU \sgshpr{} method for large-scale
multi-period DC optimal power flow (DCOPF) and report results on an
A100-SXM4-80GB GPU \cite{wang2026}. Reproduction requires separating the
mathematical map, numerical instance, implementation, validation criteria,
timer boundary, and hardware. This study asks which layers can be recovered
from the paper and public artifacts.

The mathematical path and an FP64 GPU implementation are independently
validated, including a correction to the structural equality solve. Public
models reproduce the published dimensions but not the sparse supports, and
the local hardware and timers differ from the source study. We therefore use
\emph{structural reproduction} to mean that the algorithm, implementation
structure, public-network scaling, and validation behavior are reproduced
without claiming author-instance identity or controlled paper timing.

The contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a self-contained canonical DCOPF and \sgshpr{} implementation
  contract;
  \item independent residual, physical, direct-solve, and CPU/GPU trajectory
  checks;
  \item a formal correction and stable implementation of the structural
  equality inverse;
  \item an 18-case structural ledger, an 11-case benchmark record, and
  repeated timing distributions; and
  \item live-memory and sparse-index boundary evidence with a public,
  versioned code/data release.
\end{itemize}

The displayed source model has no outage or contingency index. This report
therefore studies base-case multi-period DCOPF; N-1 contingency analysis is
outside the scope of this study.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Source paper, scope, and public data}

The source paper is the 17-page article by Wang \emph{et al.}
\cite{wang2026}. It applies HPR to the dual of a box-constrained LP, splits equality and
inequality multipliers with an sGS sweep, and uses CSR sparse products on an
A100. Its $O(1/k)$ KKT residual result follows the HPR framework
\cite{chen2026}.

Public base networks are MATPOWER 8.1
\texttt{case1354pegase}, \texttt{case2868rte}, and
\texttt{case9241pegase} \cite{matpower,josz2016}. The PEGASE/RTE provenance is described in
\cite{fliscounakis2013,josz2016}. The authors' renewable/storage placements,
time series, reserve profiles, modified ramps, storage data, matrix
construction, exact control code, and timer boundary were unavailable.
Appendix~\ref{app:recipe} gives the deterministic replacement policy.

\subsection{Mathematical formulation}

The implementation contract is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{x\in\R^n}\quad & c^{\mathsf T}x,\\
\mathrm{s.t.}\quad & A_1x=b_1,\\
& A_2x\ge b_2,\\
& \ell\le x\le u,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:primal}
\end{equation}
where $A=[A_1;A_2]$, $b=[b_1;b_2]$, and $C=[\ell,u]$. With
$D=\R^{m_1}\times\R_+^{m_2}$, the dual is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{y,z}\quad &-b^{\mathsf T}y+\delta_D(y)+\delta_C^*(-z),\\
\mathrm{s.t.}\quad &A^{\mathsf T}y+z=c.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:dual}
\end{equation}
Equality multipliers are free; inequality multipliers are nonnegative. The
canonical inequality direction is always $A_2x\ge b_2$.

The decision order is
\begin{equation}
x=(p_G,p_{RG},p_{ESS}^{dc},p_{ESS}^{ch},r^u,r^d).
\end{equation}
For $T$ periods, $N_L$ constrained lines, $N_G$ conventional generators,
$N_{RG}$ renewables, and $N_{ESS}$ storage devices,
\begin{align}
n &= T(3N_G+N_{RG}+2N_{ESS}),\\
m_1 &= T+N_{ESS},\\
m_2 &= 2TN_L+(4T-2)N_G+2TN_{ESS}+2T.
\end{align}
Power balance and terminal storage energy form $A_1$. Split branch flow,
headroom/footroom, reserve, ramp, and storage-energy rows form $A_2$; remaining
device limits form $C$.

The full KKT residual is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{R}(y,z,x)=
\begin{pmatrix}
y-\Pi_D(y-Ax+b)\\
x-\Pi_C(x-z)\\
c-A^{\mathsf T}y-z
\end{pmatrix}.
\label{eq:kkt}
\end{equation}
The paper's stopping rule separately normalizes primal feasibility, box
normal-cone consistency, and stationarity. Its first stopping block is not the
entire first block of \eqref{eq:kkt}; both maps are retained.

\subsection{Implemented algorithm and sourced control policy}

For $w=(y_1,y_2,z,x)$, the FP64 implementation follows the printed update
order. The combined $z$/primal update gives
\begin{equation}
\bar x^{k+1}=\Pi_C\!\left[x^k+\sigma(A^{\mathsf T}y^k-c)\right].
\label{eq:xproj}
\end{equation}
An equality solve computes $\bar y_1^{k+1/2}$ using $y_2^k$. Then
\begin{equation}
\bar y_2^{k+1}=\Pi_{\R_+^{m_2}}
\left[y_2^k+\lambda^{-1}(b_2/\sigma-A_2R_y)\right],
\label{eq:y2}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda$ safely bounds $\norm{A_2}_2^2$. A second equality solve uses
the new $\bar y_2^{k+1}$ to obtain $\bar y_1^{k+1}$. Finally,
\begin{align}
\widehat w^{k+1}&=2\bar w^{k+1}-w^k,\\
w^{k+1}&=\frac{1}{k+2}w^0+
          \frac{k+1}{k+2}\widehat w^{k+1}.
\label{eq:halpern}
\end{align}
The anchor $w^0$ is fixed.

Production preprocessing applies ten Ruiz equilibration passes
\cite{ruiz2001}, one simultaneous Pock--Chambolle diagonal step with
$\alpha=1$ \cite{pock2011}, and complete-vector $b$/$c$ normalization.
Initial $\sigma=1$. The DCOPF paper does not publish exact restart or
adaptive-penalty code; Appendix~\ref{app:controls} states the transferred
HPR-LP rules, formulas, guards, and source pin explicitly
\cite{chen2026}. This is a sourced reconstruction, not author-code identity.

\section{Verification}

\subsection{Structural equality correction}

Each \sgshpr{} iteration solves an equality system involving
$A_1A_1^{\mathsf T}$. Its first $T$ rows are hourly power balances and its
remaining rows enforce terminal storage energy. Every balance row has the same
structural weight, while each terminal-energy row couples to every period in
the same way. The resulting Gram matrix is diagonal except for one direction
shared by all periods. Lemma~1 exploits that structure instead of forming or
factorizing a dense matrix.

\textbf{Lemma 1 (stable inverse of the reduced equality block).}
Let $D_2\succ0$ be diagonal, $a>0$, $d$ a vector, and
$\alpha=d^{\mathsf T}D_2^{-1}d$. For
\begin{equation}
\begin{bmatrix}
aI_T & \bm{1}d^{\mathsf T}\\
d\bm{1}^{\mathsf T} & D_2
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}y_{11}\\y_{12}\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}r_{11}\\r_{12}\end{bmatrix},
\end{equation}
define
\begin{align}
\widehat r&=r_{11}-\bm{1}d^{\mathsf T}D_2^{-1}r_{12},\\
\gamma&=a-T\alpha,\qquad
\mu=T^{-1}\bm{1}^{\mathsf T}\widehat r.
\end{align}
If $\gamma>0$, the unique solution is
\begin{align}
y_{11}&=\frac{\widehat r-\mu\bm{1}}{a}
       +\frac{\mu}{\gamma}\bm{1},
\label{eq:stable-y11}\\
y_{12}&=D_2^{-1}\!\left(r_{12}-d\bm{1}^{\mathsf T}y_{11}\right).
\label{eq:stable-y12}
\end{align}

\emph{Interpretation.} The vector $\bm{1}$ is the common-across-time direction.
The adjusted right-hand side $\widehat r$ separates into its average
$\mu\bm{1}$ and a zero-mean deviation $\widehat r-\mu\bm{1}$. The rank-one
storage coupling does not act on the zero-mean part, so that part is divided by
$a$. It acts only on the average, which is divided by the corrected scalar
$\gamma$. The storage multipliers then follow from a diagonal back-substitution.
Thus an apparently dense $(T+N_{ESS})$-row solve requires only vector sums,
diagonal divisions, and scalar operations.

\emph{Proof.} Eliminating $y_{12}$ yields
$(aI_T-\alpha\bm{1}\bm{1}^{\mathsf T})y_{11}=\widehat r$.
The zero-mean subspace has eigenvalue $a$ and
$\operatorname{span}\{\bm{1}\}$ has eigenvalue
$a-T\alpha=\gamma$. Decomposing $\widehat r$ into mean and zero-mean
components gives \eqref{eq:stable-y11}; back-substitution gives
\eqref{eq:stable-y12}. \hfill$\square$

The printed expression uses the sign pattern of a plus rank-one update and
fails a direct linear-system oracle. Equation~\eqref{eq:stable-y11} avoids
forming the cancellation-prone Woodbury denominator in the solve. The code
also evaluates $\gamma$ through a positive-sum identity (Appendix
\ref{app:algebra}). On the sign fixture, the printed formula's median relative
error is 0.206; the corrected maximum relative error is
$3.03\times10^{-16}$. Across condition numbers from 1 to 25,616, the largest
normalized residual is $2.93\times10^{-14}$.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Structural reconciliation}

All 18 Table II $(m,n)$ pairs match. Zero reconstructed nnz counts match;
differences range from approximately $-8.14\%$ to $-36.66\%$. Thus the
public models reproduce the dimension algebra but not the author sparse
supports. Paper/local timing ratios are invalid before hardware differences
are considered.

snapshots, not per-solve high-water marks.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Reproduction boundary}

The canonical signs, variables, constraint families, all 18 $(m,n)$ pairs,
update order, Halpern weights, tolerance, Ruiz/Pock--Chambolle preprocessing,
initial $\sigma$, policy cadence, FP64 CSR path, and requested ALG2 selection
are reproduced. CPU/GPU trajectories and all accepted original-space checks
agree.

Author-instance identity is not reproduced. Placement, time series, reserve
and ramp edits, storage parameters, costs, PTDF reference/sparsification,
control code, source revision, precision details, and timer boundary are
missing. Equation (55) and Table II imply $m_1=T+N_{ESS}$ while Appendix A
prose implies $T(1+N_{ESS})$; the rank-one sign is inconsistent with the Schur
complement; and the security-constrained label lacks an explicit outage model.

\subsection{Limitations and next steps}

The major uncertainties are instance construction and external performance,
not whether the local code can execute. Timing cannot disentangle algorithm,
support, implementation, hardware, runtime, or stopwatch effects. The T6 CPU
track is censored and gives no median. T16/T24/T32 give safe boundary decisions,
not solve times. Memory APIs limit peak attribution. The adaptive policy is a
pinned HPR-LP transfer rather than unpublished DCOPF author code.

The most valuable follow-up is author-instance reconciliation: obtain modified
cases, profiles, placements, parameters, construction code, precision,
control logic, and timer rules; first match $(m,n,\mathrm{nnz})$, then
objective/KKT fingerprints, then an equivalent A100 boundary. If those
artifacts remain unavailable, 64-bit sparse indices, partitioned operators,
or lower-peak assembly would be a new scalability study rather than a
correction to this result.

\section{Conclusion}

This study reconstructs the path from canonical DCOPF to a resident FP64 GPU
\sgshpr{} solver and corrects a quantitatively material rank-one sign error.
CPU/GPU trajectories and original-unit constraints agree across the accepted
public cases, but every reconstructed sparse support differs from the source
paper and one required CPU solve is censored at its time limit. The evidence
therefore establishes structural reproduction, not author-instance identity
or controlled performance reproduction.

\section{Solver Implementations and Measurement Boundaries}
\label{app:solvers}

\textbf{HiGHS reference.} SciPy/HiGHS supplies the independent objective via
\texttt{linprog(method='highs-ds')} with presolve, $10^{-9}$ primal and dual
feasibility tolerances, and a 3,600-s limit \cite{scipy2020,highs2018}. Its
reported core time is the \texttt{linprog} call, including interface/model
setup and the solve.

\textbf{CPU oracle.} The CPU \sgshpr{} implementation uses SciPy sparse
operators in FP64, a zero initial state, both equality sweeps,
original-coordinate recovery, and independent residual and physical checks.
Its measured time begins after model, preconditioner, and workspace preparation
and includes the iterative solve and scheduled residual work.

\textbf{GPU implementation.} The target path uses CuPy 14.1.1 arrays, FP64 CSR
matrices, resident state, and reused workspaces \cite{okuta2017}. A low-level
descriptor records \texttt{CUSPARSE\_SPMV\_CSR\_ALG2}; normal and transpose
probes verify the selected path. NVIDIA documents CSR ALG2 as deterministic for
non-transpose SpMV \cite{cusparse}. Transfer ledgers reject unexpected
full-state copies inside the iteration loop. The GPU clock uses the same
post-preparation iterative/residual boundary as the CPU path.

CUDA initialization, model construction, preprocessing, first-run compilation,
allocation, transfers, solver initialization, nested residuals, recovery, and
complete runner wall time are separately named in the released timing ledger.
Nested residual time is already part of loop time and is not added again.

\section{Preprocessing, Restart, and Penalty Controls}
\label{app:controls}

The sGS multiplier metric for $\Delta y=(\Delta y_1,\Delta y_2)$ is
\begin{align}
v&=A_1^{\mathsf T}\Delta y_1+A_2^{\mathsf T}\Delta y_2,\\
Q_y(\Delta y)&=(A_1v)^{\mathsf T}
(A_1A_1^{\mathsf T})^{-1}(A_1v)
+\lambda\norm{\Delta y_2}^2.
\end{align}
The restart merit is
\begin{equation}
\widetilde R^2=
\norm{\Delta x}^2/\sigma+
2(A\Delta x)^{\mathsf T}\Delta y+
\sigma Q_y(\Delta y).
\end{equation}
Policy checks occur every 100 iterations; the first checkpoint forces a
restart. Later checkpoints restart for sufficient decay
$\widetilde R\le0.2\widetilde R_{ref}$, necessary decay with no local
progress
$\widetilde R\le0.6\widetilde R_{ref}$ and
$\widetilde R>\widetilde R_{prev}$, or a long inner loop
$k_{inner}\ge0.2k_{total}$. An accepted restart copies the proximal point to
anchor and current state and resets the inner counter.

With reference and candidate states,
\begin{align}
\Delta_x&=\norm{x_c-x_r}, &
\Delta_y&=\sqrt{Q_y(y_c-y_r)}, \label{eq:policy-moves}\\
\sigma_{new}&=\Delta_x/\Delta_y. \label{eq:policy-sigma}
\end{align}
Both movements must lie strictly in $(10^{-16},10^{12})$, and the normalized
dual/primal infeasibility ratio in $(10^{-8},10^8)$. Guard failure resets
$\sigma$ to 1. The policy source is HPR-LP commit
\texttt{1941fbcfbf2dae14e4a439b22f0ea1e1c05f4a29}
\cite{chen2026}.

\section{Algebraic Stability and Conditioning}
\label{app:algebra}

The positive-sum implementation of the reduced eigenvalue is
\begin{equation}
\gamma=G+R+\sum_s
\frac{(\eta_s^{ch}-1/\eta_s^{dc})^2}
{(\eta_s^{ch})^2+1/(\eta_s^{dc})^2},
\end{equation}
which avoids subtracting nearly equal $a$ and $T\alpha$. Nonpositive or
