\begin{abstract}
We assess whether the mathematical and computational route of a published
GPU symmetric Gauss--Seidel Halpern-accelerated proximal-reflection
(\sgshpr{}) method for multi-period DC optimal power flow can be reconstructed
and validated without the authors' instances or code. The canonical LP,
paper-order updates, stopping and KKT maps, structural equality solve, FP64 CPU
oracle, and resident NVIDIA DGX Spark GPU path were implemented and checked.
A sign inconsistency in the printed rank-one inverse is corrected; the
corrected solve has maximum relative error $3.03\times10^{-16}$, whereas the
printed sign has median relative error 0.206. Public MATPOWER reconstructions
match all 18 published row and variable dimensions but none of the nonzero
counts. Eleven rows were allocated and GPU-validated; ten also passed the
required CPU track. The remaining CPU attempt reached the frozen 3,600-s
limit. Three later rows stopped before allocation: T16 exceeded observed live
memory budgets despite fitting the nominal 80\% reference, T24 exceeded a
conservative int32 planning envelope while its count-only nnz did not, and T32
exceeded int32 under both measures. Under a preregistered project-specific
taxonomy, the result is \classresult{}. No paper-time reproduction or local
speedup is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
DC optimal power flow, Halpern iteration, proximal reflection, symmetric

Spark.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}

Wang \emph{et al.} propose a GPU \sgshpr{} method for large-scale
multi-period DC optimal power flow (DCOPF) and report results on an
A100-SXM4-80GB GPU \cite{wang2026}. Reproducing that work requires more than
running a related optimizer: the mathematical map, instance identity,
implementation path, validation criteria, timing boundary, and hardware
conditions must be separated. This study asks which of those layers can be
recovered from the paper and public artifacts, and which conclusions remain
unsupported.

The project froze an A--E decision framework before final synthesis:
A exact, B near-exact, C mathematical, D structural, and E partial. This is a
project-specific evidence-resolution taxonomy, not an ACM artifact badge or a
replacement for the standard repeatability/reproducibility/replicability
terminology \cite{acm2020}. Structural reproduction means that the
mathematical path, implementation structure, public-network scaling,
validation, and resource behavior are reproduced while author-instance
identity and controlled paper timing are not.

The principal result is \classresult{}. The mathematics and an FP64 GPU
implementation are validated, but the author instances are unavailable, all
18 reconstructed sparse supports differ, and the frozen large-run campaign
contains a required CPU timeout. A separately implemented validation checker
records that the protocol and evidence are internally consistent; it does not
convert the Stage 8 scientific decision from FAIL to PASS.

The contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a self-contained canonical DCOPF and \sgshpr{} implementation
  contract;
  \item independent projection, residual, physical, spectral, direct-solve,
  and CPU/GPU trajectory checks;
  \item a formal correction and stable implementation of the structural
  equality inverse;
  \item an 18-row structural ledger, an 11-row allocated benchmark record,
  and repeated timing distributions;
  \item fail-closed live-memory and sparse-index evidence with the planning
  and count-only interpretations kept distinct; and
  \item a public, versioned code/data release with machine-readable
  provenance and regeneration instructions.
\end{itemize}

The displayed source model has no outage or contingency index. This report
therefore studies base-case multi-period DCOPF. N-1 SCOPF is new work and
Stage 10 remains locked.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Source paper, scope, and public data}

The source paper is the 17-page article by Wang \emph{et al.}
\cite{wang2026}; the supplied PDF has SHA-256
\begin{center}
\scriptsize
\url{7e9791646401e11bfddf9ebed6bd94491ed0b592744581edd851ddbf5e20dba4}.
\end{center}
The paper applies HPR to the dual of a box-constrained LP, splits equality and
inequality multipliers with an sGS sweep, and uses CSR sparse products on an
A100. Its $O(1/k)$ KKT residual result follows the HPR framework
\cite{chen2026}.

Public base networks are MATPOWER 8.1
\texttt{case1354pegase}, \texttt{case2868rte}, and
\texttt{case9241pegase}, pinned by upstream commit, Git blob, and SHA-256
\cite{matpower,josz2016}. The PEGASE/RTE provenance is described in
\cite{fliscounakis2013,josz2016}. The authors' renewable/storage placements,
time series, reserve profiles, modified ramps, storage data, matrix
construction, exact control code, and timer boundary were unavailable.
Appendix~\ref{app:recipe} gives the deterministic replacement policy.

\subsection{Mathematical formulation}

The implementation contract is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{x\in\R^n}\quad & c^{\mathsf T}x,\\
\mathrm{s.t.}\quad & A_1x=b_1,\\
& A_2x\ge b_2,\\
& \ell\le x\le u,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:primal}
\end{equation}
where $A=[A_1;A_2]$, $b=[b_1;b_2]$, and $C=[\ell,u]$. With
$D=\R^{m_1}\times\R_+^{m_2}$, the dual is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{y,z}\quad &-b^{\mathsf T}y+\delta_D(y)+\delta_C^*(-z),\\
\mathrm{s.t.}\quad &A^{\mathsf T}y+z=c.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:dual}
\end{equation}
Equality multipliers are free; inequality multipliers are nonnegative. The
canonical inequality direction is always $A_2x\ge b_2$.

The decision order is
\begin{equation}
x=(p_G,p_{RG},p_{ESS}^{dc},p_{ESS}^{ch},r^u,r^d).
\end{equation}
For $T$ periods, $N_L$ constrained lines, $N_G$ conventional generators,
$N_{RG}$ renewables, and $N_{ESS}$ storage devices,
\begin{align}
n &= T(3N_G+N_{RG}+2N_{ESS}),\\
m_1 &= T+N_{ESS},\\
m_2 &= 2TN_L+(4T-2)N_G+2TN_{ESS}+2T.
\end{align}
Power balance and terminal storage energy form $A_1$. Split branch flow,
headroom/footroom, reserve, ramp, and storage-energy rows form $A_2$; remaining
device limits form $C$.

The full KKT residual is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{R}(y,z,x)=
\begin{pmatrix}
y-\Pi_D(y-Ax+b)\\
x-\Pi_C(x-z)\\
c-A^{\mathsf T}y-z
\end{pmatrix}.
\label{eq:kkt}
\end{equation}
The paper's stopping rule separately normalizes primal feasibility, box
normal-cone consistency, and stationarity. Its first stopping block is not the
entire first block of \eqref{eq:kkt}; both maps are retained.

\subsection{Implemented algorithm and sourced control policy}

For $w=(y_1,y_2,z,x)$, the FP64 implementation follows the printed update
order. The combined $z$/primal update gives
\begin{equation}
\bar x^{k+1}=\Pi_C\!\left[x^k+\sigma(A^{\mathsf T}y^k-c)\right].
\label{eq:xproj}
\end{equation}
An equality solve computes $\bar y_1^{k+1/2}$ using $y_2^k$. Then
\begin{equation}
\bar y_2^{k+1}=\Pi_{\R_+^{m_2}}
\left[y_2^k+\lambda^{-1}(b_2/\sigma-A_2R_y)\right],
\label{eq:y2}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda$ safely bounds $\norm{A_2}_2^2$. A second equality solve uses
the new $\bar y_2^{k+1}$ to obtain $\bar y_1^{k+1}$. Finally,
\begin{align}
\widehat w^{k+1}&=2\bar w^{k+1}-w^k,\\
w^{k+1}&=\frac{1}{k+2}w^0+
          \frac{k+1}{k+2}\widehat w^{k+1}.
\label{eq:halpern}
\end{align}
The anchor $w^0$ is fixed.

Production preprocessing applies ten Ruiz equilibration passes
\cite{ruiz2001}, one simultaneous Pock--Chambolle diagonal step with
$\alpha=1$ \cite{pock2011}, and complete-vector $b$/$c$ normalization.
Initial $\sigma=1$. The DCOPF paper does not publish exact restart or
adaptive-penalty code; Appendix~\ref{app:controls} states the transferred
HPR-LP rules, formulas, guards, and source pin explicitly
\cite{chen2026}. This is a sourced reconstruction, not author-code identity.

\subsection{CPU, GPU, and reference solvers}

The CPU oracle uses SciPy sparse operators in FP64, zero initial state, both
equality sweeps, original-coordinate recovery, and independent residual and
physical checks. SciPy/HiGHS supplies the reference objective via
\texttt{linprog(method='highs-ds')} with presolve, $10^{-9}$ primal and dual
feasibility tolerances, and a 3,600-s limit \cite{scipy2020,highs2018}.

The GPU path uses CuPy 14.1.1 arrays, FP64 CSR matrices, resident state, and
reused workspaces \cite{okuta2017}. A low-level descriptor records
\texttt{CUSPARSE\_SPMV\_CSR\_ALG2}; normal and transpose probes verify the
selected path. NVIDIA documents CSR ALG2 as deterministic for non-transpose
SpMV \cite{cusparse}. Transfer ledgers reject unexpected full-state copies
inside the iteration loop.

\subsection{Acceptance and timing design}

Every accepted candidate must meet normalized stopping blocks
$\le5\times10^{-5}$, raw KKT $\le10^{-2}$, physical violation
$\le10^{-2}$ MW/MWh, and scaled objective gap to HiGHS
$\le2\times10^{-4}$. Stage 7--8 requires HiGHS, CPU FP64, and GPU FP64 unless
a later continuation explicitly skips CPU without rewriting the original
decision.

Each successful timing track has one correctness solve, one warm-up, and at
least five measured solves. Relative range above 0.2 escalates the track to
nine repeats. The report retains median, minimum, maximum, standard deviation,
and interquartile range (IQR). HiGHS time includes the SciPy interface/model
setup and solve; CPU/GPU times use separately prepared solver workspaces and
include their solver-specific iteration/residual boundary. These definitions
are not interchangeable. The T6 CPU event is one censored correctness
attempt, not a timing median.

\subsection{Structural equality correction}

\textbf{Lemma 1 (stable inverse of the reduced equality block).}
Let $D_2\succ0$ be diagonal, $a>0$, $d$ a vector, and
$\alpha=d^{\mathsf T}D_2^{-1}d$. For
\begin{equation}
\begin{bmatrix}
aI_T & \bm{1}d^{\mathsf T}\\
d\bm{1}^{\mathsf T} & D_2
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}y_{11}\\y_{12}\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}r_{11}\\r_{12}\end{bmatrix},
\end{equation}
define
\begin{align}
\widehat r&=r_{11}-\bm{1}d^{\mathsf T}D_2^{-1}r_{12},\\
\gamma&=a-T\alpha,\qquad
\mu=T^{-1}\bm{1}^{\mathsf T}\widehat r.
\end{align}
If $\gamma>0$, the unique solution is
\begin{align}
y_{11}&=\frac{\widehat r-\mu\bm{1}}{a}
       +\frac{\mu}{\gamma}\bm{1},
\label{eq:stable-y11}\\
y_{12}&=D_2^{-1}\!\left(r_{12}-d\bm{1}^{\mathsf T}y_{11}\right).
\label{eq:stable-y12}
\end{align}

\emph{Proof.} Eliminating $y_{12}$ yields
$(aI_T-\alpha\bm{1}\bm{1}^{\mathsf T})y_{11}=\widehat r$.
The zero-mean subspace has eigenvalue $a$ and
$\operatorname{span}\{\bm{1}\}$ has eigenvalue
$a-T\alpha=\gamma$. Decomposing $\widehat r$ into mean and zero-mean
components gives \eqref{eq:stable-y11}; back-substitution gives
\eqref{eq:stable-y12}. \hfill$\square$

The printed expression uses the sign pattern of a plus rank-one update and
fails a direct linear-system oracle. Equation~\eqref{eq:stable-y11} avoids
forming the cancellation-prone Woodbury denominator in the solve. The code
also evaluates $\gamma$ through a positive-sum identity (Appendix
\ref{app:algebra}). On the sign fixture, the printed formula's median relative
error is 0.206; the corrected maximum relative error is
$3.03\times10^{-16}$. Across condition numbers from 1 to 25,616, the largest
normalized residual is $2.93\times10^{-14}$.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{What was and was not reproduced}

The canonical signs, variables, constraint families, all 18 $(m,n)$ pairs,
update order, Halpern weights, tolerance, Ruiz/Pock--Chambolle preprocessing,
initial $\sigma$, policy cadence, FP64 CSR path, and requested ALG2 selection
are reproduced. CPU/GPU trajectories and all accepted original-space checks
agree.

Author-instance identity is not reproduced. Placement, time series, reserve
and ramp edits, storage parameters, costs, PTDF reference/sparsification,
control code, source revision, precision details, and timer boundary are
missing. Equation (55) and Table II imply $m_1=T+N_{ESS}$ while Appendix A
prose implies $T(1+N_{ESS})$; the rank-one sign is inconsistent with the Schur
complement; and the security-constrained label lacks an explicit outage model.

\subsection{Classification and uncertainty}

Class A is rejected because author artifacts, hardware, and exact outputs are
unavailable. B is rejected because every nnz differs and timing is
uncontrolled. C is achieved mathematically but understates the executed
public-network scaling. E understates the validated solver and complete
structural ledger. Under the frozen internal taxonomy, D is the narrowest
defensible label.

The major uncertainties are instance construction and external performance,
not whether the local code can execute. Timing cannot disentangle algorithm,
support, implementation, hardware, runtime, or stopwatch effects. The T6 CPU
track is censored and gives no median. T16/T24/T32 give safe classifications,
not solve times. Memory APIs limit peak attribution. The adaptive policy is a
pinned HPR-LP transfer rather than unpublished DCOPF author code.

The most valuable follow-up is author-instance reconciliation: obtain modified
cases, profiles, placements, parameters, construction code, precision,
control logic, and timer rules; first match $(m,n,\mathrm{nnz})$, then
objective/KKT fingerprints, then an equivalent A100 boundary. If those
artifacts remain unavailable, 64-bit sparse indices, partitioned operators,
or lower-peak assembly would be a new scalability study rather than a
correction to this result.

\section{Conclusion}

This study reconstructs and validates the paper's path from canonical DCOPF
to a resident FP64 GPU \sgshpr{} solver. It records a quantitatively material
rank-one sign error, separates stopping from full KKT validation, verifies
CPU/GPU trajectories, retains repeated timing dispersion, and distinguishes
three different preallocation outcomes. The evidence also contains a genuine
required-track timeout and complete sparse-support mismatch. The result is
therefore \classresult{}---stronger than a component demonstration and
narrower than an exact, near-exact, or controlled performance reproduction.

\section{Preprocessing, Restart, Penalty, and Timing Definitions}
\label{app:controls}

The sGS multiplier metric for $\Delta y=(\Delta y_1,\Delta y_2)$ is
\begin{align}
v&=A_1^{\mathsf T}\Delta y_1+A_2^{\mathsf T}\Delta y_2,\\
Q_y(\Delta y)&=(A_1v)^{\mathsf T}
(A_1A_1^{\mathsf T})^{-1}(A_1v)
+\lambda\norm{\Delta y_2}^2.
\end{align}
The restart merit is
\begin{equation}
\widetilde R^2=
\norm{\Delta x}^2/\sigma+
2(A\Delta x)^{\mathsf T}\Delta y+
\sigma Q_y(\Delta y).
\end{equation}
Policy checks occur every 100 iterations; the first checkpoint forces a
restart. Later checkpoints restart for sufficient decay
$\widetilde R\le0.2\widetilde R_{ref}$, necessary decay with no local
progress
$\widetilde R\le0.6\widetilde R_{ref}$ and
$\widetilde R>\widetilde R_{prev}$, or a long inner loop
$k_{inner}\ge0.2k_{total}$. An accepted restart copies the proximal point to
anchor and current state and resets the inner counter.

With reference and candidate states,
\begin{align}
\Delta_x&=\norm{x_c-x_r}, &
\Delta_y&=\sqrt{Q_y(y_c-y_r)}, \label{eq:policy-moves}\\
\sigma_{new}&=\Delta_x/\Delta_y. \label{eq:policy-sigma}
\end{align}
Both movements must lie strictly in $(10^{-16},10^{12})$, and the normalized
dual/primal infeasibility ratio in $(10^{-8},10^8)$. Guard failure resets
$\sigma$ to 1. The policy source is HPR-LP commit
\texttt{1941fbcfbf2dae14e4a439b22f0ea1e1c05f4a29}
\cite{chen2026}.

HiGHS core time is the SciPy \texttt{linprog} call, including interface/model
setup and solve. CPU/GPU \sgshpr{} times begin after their solver-specific
model/preconditioner/workspace preparation and include the iterative solve and
scheduled residual work. CUDA initialization, model construction,
preprocessing, first-run compilation, allocation, transfers, solver
initialization, iteration, nested residuals, recovery, and complete runner
wall time are separately named in
\pathcode{results/tables/stage_9_timing_decomposition.csv}. Nested residual
time is part of loop time and must not be added again.
